Working proposal · Team collaboration

When every teammate has an agent, how do we still reach one decision?

Our current workflow produces polished ideas quickly, but the discussion fragments across chats, revisions, and “final-final” documents. We need one visual place where people can understand, challenge, and converge.

Author: MayaStatus: seeking feedbackDraft 01

The problem

Today, feedback arrives after the document has already forked. A teammate responds to an old version in chat; another creates a counter-proposal with their own agent. The team spends its time reconciling artifacts instead of resolving the actual decision.

Success metric: from a baseline of 18 weekly clarification loops, reduce “where is the latest version?” and clarification loops by 50% within 60 days of adoption.

First, make the idea visible locally

Before Maya shares anything, she works with an agent in local edit mode. A rough brief becomes a testable product concept, its trade-offs, and an explanation people can react to—not a wall of markdown or a screenshot with no context.

The same link updates; comments stay attached and show the author’s reply or a visible before/after when the referenced text changes.

Proposed rollout

Rollout: start with product and engineering planning documents, then expand to customer-facing proposals after two successful team cycles.

  • Keep every comment anchored to the source claim or visual.
  • Use the agent to summarize agreement, disagreement, and missing evidence.
  • Require a human owner to make the final trade-off call.
Open decision: Should the first rollout optimize for speed of consensus, or for a stronger audit trail of why a decision changed?

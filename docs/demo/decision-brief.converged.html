Working proposal · Team collaboration

When every teammate has an agent, how do we still reach one decision?

We generate more proposals than ever and read fewer of them. This brief argues for one visual place where a team can understand, challenge, and converge — instead of trading polished documents that never answer each other.

Author: Maya Status: seeking feedback Draft 01

“Everyone generates. Few truly read. Two confident documents end up talking past each other — V1, V2, V3, none of them converging.”

The cost is not writing time. It is the decision that never gets made.

The problem

Feedback arrives after the document has already forked. A teammate replies to an old version in chat; another returns a counter-proposal written with their own agent. The team then spends its time reconciling artifacts instead of resolving the actual decision.

Success metric: We currently see 18 clarification loops per week; reduce that count by 50% within two rollout cycles, while also reducing “where is the latest version?” messages and making decisions faster.

First, make the idea visible — locally

Before Maya shares anything, she works with her agent in local edit mode. A rough brief becomes a testable product concept with its evidence and its open trade-off — not a wall of markdown, and not a screenshot with no context.

The collaboration loop

One shared HTML document becomes the place the conversation happens — and the place it resolves.

Where the human stays in the loop

Agents do the drafting and the synthesis. The decision boundary is explicit and it belongs to a person.

Proposed rollout

Rollout: start with internal product and engineering planning documents, then expand to customer-facing proposals after two successful cycles.

  • Keep every comment anchored to the source claim, table, or diagram.
  • Use the agent to summarize agreement, disagreement, and missing evidence.
  • Require the document owner to make the final trade-off call.
Open decision: Should the first rollout optimize for speed of consensus, or for a stronger audit trail of why a decision changed?
